# Content policy and data handling for Persona1's model

Checked September 26, 2026. This is source-based product research, not legal advice or a live behavior test. No model request was made.

## Answer

**Do not treat `opencode-go/deepseek-v4.1-flash` as cleared for a public romantic iMessage companion.** OpenCode Go lists the model, but says clients should send typical **coding-agent traffic**; OpenCode's terms limit the Services to the customer's **own internal use**, not on behalf of or for the benefit of a third party. A service that texts members of the public as a girlfriend-like persona is not demonstrably within either condition. Obtain written authorization from OpenCode or use a provider whose terms explicitly cover the intended public use before launch. [Go guide, “Where can I use it?”](https://opencode.ai/v2/docs/console/go#where-can-i-use-it); [OpenCode Terms, “What are the basics of using OpenCode?”](https://opencode.ai/legal/terms-of-service#what-are-the-basics-of-using-opencode)

## Applicable rules

- OpenCode Go is a hosted provider accessible with an OpenCode Go API key; its published model list and endpoint table include **DeepSeek V4.1 Flash** (`deepseek-v4.1-flash`) at `https://opencode.ai/zen/go/v1/chat/completions`. These facts establish the OpenCode gateway, not the identity or geographic location of its downstream inference host. [Go guide, “How it works,” “Endpoints”](https://opencode.ai/v2/docs/console/go)
- OpenCode's Terms of Use are with **Anomaly Innovations, Inc.** and cover its hosted inference offering. They restrict use to your own internal use, prohibit using the Services for the benefit of third parties, and forbid content that is “obscene” or “otherwise objectionable” and representing AI output as human-generated. They do **not** expressly approve romantic role-play between adults, or define where affection ends and prohibited obscenity begins. An AI disclosure matters, but does not remove the public-use restriction. [OpenCode Terms, opening, “What are the basics,” “Are there restrictions”](https://opencode.ai/legal/terms-of-service)
- OpenCode Go additionally says it is designed for OpenCode and other **coding agents** producing similar requests, asks clients to send “typical coding agent traffic,” and says it monitors traffic for abuse degrading other users' experience. A non-coding romantic chat is outside the documented intended workload; whether Go would specifically reject it is **unconfirmed**. [Go guide, “Where can I use it?”](https://opencode.ai/v2/docs/console/go#where-can-i-use-it)
- **If DeepSeek's own Open Platform terms govern the downstream serving arrangement:** DeepSeek's Open Platform terms allow API integration into applications for external end users but require those end users to comply with DeepSeek's general Terms, require the developer to manage their conduct and disclose AI-generated output, and put end-user privacy-notice obligations on the developer. These permissions do not override OpenCode Go's separate terms. Applicability of DeepSeek's direct developer agreement to an OpenCode Go customer is **unconfirmed**. [DeepSeek Open Platform Terms §§1.1, 3.1–3.3, 8.1](https://cdn.deepseek.com/policies/en-US/deepseek-open-platform-terms-of-service.html)
- DeepSeek's general Terms §3.4(5) prohibit creating or promoting obscene, pornographic, or sexually explicit content or chatbots, explicitly giving **adult chatbots** as an example. §3.4(8) also prohibits bots designed to appeal especially to minors or embody a minor (Persona1 is stated to be 24). The text does **not** explicitly prohibit non-explicit, consensual romance between adults, but does **not** grant an exception for explicit sexual content merely because the participants are adults. The currently linked `en-US` general-terms URL returned a Korean-language document when checked; these descriptions translate its §3.4, so verify the language/version with DeepSeek if contractual reliance is necessary. [DeepSeek Terms §3.4](https://cdn.deepseek.com/policies/en-US/deepseek-terms-of-use.html); [DeepSeek Open Platform Terms §3.1 (incorporation)](https://cdn.deepseek.com/policies/en-US/deepseek-open-platform-terms-of-service.html)

## Refusals and filtering

- DeepSeek says it may review **inputs and outputs** and build risk filters, and its Open Platform terms permit warning, restricting service, suspension, or deleting content in response to suspected breaches. These are moderation powers, not a promise that all disallowed requests will be caught or that any particular wording will be returned. [DeepSeek Terms §3.3](https://cdn.deepseek.com/policies/en-US/deepseek-terms-of-use.html); [DeepSeek Open Platform Terms §7.2](https://cdn.deepseek.com/policies/en-US/deepseek-open-platform-terms-of-service.html)
- DeepSeek's _direct_ Chat Completions API defines `choices[].finish_reason = "content_filter"` for content omitted by a content-filter flag; `message.content` may be `null`. An ordinary model-written refusal would instead be message text (the API documentation does not prescribe a refusal phrase or separate refusal field). The Go guide advertises an OpenAI-compatible chat-completions endpoint for this model, but does **not** guarantee that Go forwards DeepSeek's exact `finish_reason`, that all refusals are flagged, or what error shape a gateway-side rejection uses: **unconfirmed for OpenCode Go**. Handle a missing/empty completion, a filter finish reason _if surfaced_, errors, and a natural-language refusal without assuming one sole signal. [DeepSeek Chat Completions API, response schema](https://api-docs.deepseek.com/api/create-chat-completion/); [Go guide, “Endpoints”](https://opencode.ai/v2/docs/console/go#endpoints)

## Conversation content and privacy notice

- The conversation is submitted to **Anomaly Innovations, Inc. / OpenCode Go** at the OpenCode endpoint. OpenCode's privacy policy describes conversations/prompts as passed to an upstream provider “to provide services” and “not stored”; the paid-account Terms say OpenCode uses content to provide services, comply with law, enforce policies, and keep the service safe, while saying it will not retain content. The Terms separately reserve using content to improve services for **unpaid** accounts; do not apply that exception automatically to this paid Go subscription. [Go guide, “How it works,” “Endpoints”](https://opencode.ai/v2/docs/console/go); [OpenCode Privacy Policy, “Categories of Personal Data We Collect”](https://opencode.ai/legal/privacy-policy); [OpenCode Terms, “Your IP”](https://opencode.ai/legal/terms-of-service#your-ip)
- The Go-specific **DeepSeek V4.1 Flash** privacy row says “Model training: Not used” and “Data retention: 0 days*”; its footnote says the **DeepSeek zero-data-retention agreement is renewed monthly**, with the current agreement valid **through September 30, 2026**. This is a dated, Go-specific assurance, not a perpetual or across-the-board DeepSeek guarantee; recheck it before use and after renewal. It does not establish that transient processing, caching, security metadata, the app's local transcript, or legally required records are deleted at the same instant. [Go guide, “Privacy”](https://opencode.ai/v2/docs/console/go#privacy); [OpenCode Privacy Policy, “Data Retention”](https://opencode.ai/legal/privacy-policy)
- OpenCode's Go guide names **DeepSeek** as the party to its monthly ZDR agreement and links to DeepSeek's own pricing for these models. DeepSeek's Open Platform terms identify its own platform operator as **Hangzhou DeepSeek Artificial Intelligence Co., Ltd.** But neither Go's documentation nor its terms explicitly identify that legal entity as the _actual operator of every Go inference request_ or say whether a separate hosting subcontractor is involved. **The exact downstream recipient/legal entity and its processing country for this Go route are unconfirmed.** Name **OpenCode (Anomaly Innovations, Inc.)** in the notice; mention **DeepSeek as the identified model/ZDR counterparty**, with the precise processing entity and location pending written confirmation. Do not claim “processed in China” solely because DeepSeek's direct-service privacy policy says its own servers are in China: that policy excludes end users of developers' downstream applications, and the Go routing location is not published. [Go guide, “Privacy,” “Background,” “Endpoints”](https://opencode.ai/v2/docs/console/go); [DeepSeek Open Platform Terms, opening, §5.5](https://cdn.deepseek.com/policies/en-US/deepseek-open-platform-terms-of-service.html); [DeepSeek Privacy Policy, opening and storage-location section](https://cdn.deepseek.com/policies/en-US/deepseek-privacy-policy.html)
